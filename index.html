About

I study how digital systems change the way people learn and think, and I build the tools to see it.

I trained as an engineer and computer scientist at Bombay University, then spent seven years at the Tata Institute of Social Sciences studying how technology reaches government schools. On CLIx, I ran the Open edX learning platforms, wrote interactive maths tools and trained teachers in Rajasthan, Chhattisgarh, Telangana and Mizoram. I co-authored the outcome studies of ITE, a project-based learning programme with Tata Trusts, and designed the research for AGSE, a study of 1:1 Chromebooks in two schools in Gwalior and Indore. Most recently, I led field research for the Digital Classroom Project in more than 60 government schools in Madhya Pradesh.

Since 2024 I have managed the Safexpress Centre for Data, Learning and Decision Sciences at Ashoka University. There, mathematicians, statisticians and computer scientists study AI/ML models on state-of-the-art computing infrastructure, and I get a glimpse of that research from behind the scenes.

In my spare time I help organise T4E, the international conference on technology for education, and I have worked with Tata Trusts as an evaluation consultant, writing the baseline, midline and endline reports for its ITE programme across 100+ government schools and 10,000+ students in the flood-prone Terai region of eastern Uttar Pradesh. I am also drawn to mathematical art, especially tessellations.

My questions have shifted from whether a technology works in a classroom to what it does to the people using it: how generative AI changes who does the thinking, how communities form on messaging platforms, and how culture lives in digital images. I am looking for PhD positions starting in 2027.

Research

Four questions I keep returning to.

Thinking agency and generative AI

When students read and write with AI, who is doing the knowing? I am interested in cognitive offloading and in how learners keep ownership of their own thinking.

Learning where resources are thin

Devices, connectivity and language in government schools and rural communities across the Global South, and who gets left out when a technology arrives.

Platforms and online communities

How people build communities on messaging and social platforms: who speaks, who replies, and how the rhythm of a group follows the rhythm of their lives.

Culture, creativity and heritage

Computational methods for reading images, film and archives, used to explain why certain forms move people.

Recent tools

Small tools I have built, for my own research and for the research community.

WhatsApp Chat Analyser

Turns an exported WhatsApp chat into activity charts, a reply network and measures of conversation dynamics such as turn-taking, burstiness and weekly routine. It grew out of my study of teacher communities. The chat never leaves your device.

T4E Format Check

Checks a paper against the Springer proceedings template before submission: page limits by paper type, author block, ORCID check digits, headings, figures and references, with plain-language fixes.
